Barren plateaus

How fast do gradients vanish as random circuits get wider and deeper?

The gradient variance drops 27-fold from 2 to 8 qubits, then stops falling in this sweep. At 8 qubits it is still dropping at 50 layers, which suggests the wider circuits weren't deep enough to behave fully randomly.

Results

Each point is the variance of ∂⟨Z₀Z₁⟩/∂θ₀ over 200 random circuits with random angles. The dashed line is a log-linear fit over all six widths (decay factor 0.71 per qubit). It describes the trend only loosely, because the points flatten after 8 qubits.

Method
  1. Random circuit. Ry(π/4) on every qubit, then each layer applies a randomly chosen Rx, Ry or Rz to every qubit, followed by a ladder of CZ gates (McClean et al., 2018).
  2. Random angles. Every angle uniform in [0, 2π).
  3. Gradient. d⟨Z₀Z₁⟩/dθ₀ for the first angle, by the adjoint method (exact).
  4. Variance. Over 200 circuits, at 2–12 qubits (at 50 layers) and 1–50 layers (at 8 qubits).

Caveats
  • The "too shallow" explanation for the flattening is an interpretation. A deeper sweep at 10–12 qubits would test it; we didn't run one.
  • 200 circuits per point. Variance estimates from heavy-tailed gradients are themselves noisy.
