Training under depolarizing noise

How much accuracy does hardware-like noise cost a trained model, and can a model still learn with the noise present?

A trained 17-qubit QNN goes from 85.9% to 77.0% at p = 0.01, and to chance (50.1%) at p = 0.03. Smaller 3×3 models trained with the noise present still learn, but that comparison is confounded.

Method
  1. Noise channel. A single-qubit depolarizing channel with probability p after every moment of the model circuit (circuit.with_noise).
  2. Trajectories. Each expectation averages 50 quantum trajectories (tfq.layers.NoisyPQC).
  3. Noisy evaluation, 4×4. The angles saved by Experiment 1, loaded into a noisy model, tested on 500 images per seed.
  4. Noisy training, 3×3. Parameter-shift gradients through noisy trajectories cost about 65 noisy circuits per image, which was too slow at 17 qubits (about 400 s per 64 images per epoch in a test run). So these models use 3×3 images: 200 training and 200 test images, 3 epochs, batch 16, learning rate 0.02.

Caveats
  • The two curves are different models. Don't read the gap between them as a benefit of noise-aware training.
  • Every noisy estimate carries trajectory-sampling error on top of the channel itself (50 trajectories).
  • The 3×3 input caps any model at 84.0%.
