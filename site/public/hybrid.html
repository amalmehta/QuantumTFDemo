Experiment 2 of 4

Quantum readout vs a classical head

Does reading out every qubit and adding a small dense layer help the same circuit?

Yes. The same circuit with an 8-unit dense head reaches 90.6%, up 3.8 points from the single-readout QNN's 86.8%, and matches the classical network.

Results

The hybrid model is ahead from the first epoch, and its spread across seeds is about a quarter of the QNN's (±0.6 vs ±2.3 points). Same data splits, seeds and circuit as Experiment 1.

Method
  1. Same circuit. The 17-qubit Farhi–Neven circuit with its 32 trainable angles.
  2. Read out every qubit. ⟨Z⟩ on all 17 qubits instead of only the readout qubit.
  3. Classical head. Those 17 values feed Dense(8, ReLU) → Dense(1, tanh): 153 classical parameters on top of the 32 quantum ones.
  4. Train jointly. Same loss, optimizer, data and seeds as Experiment 1. Gradients flow through the head into the circuit.

Caveats
  • The gain can't be credited to the circuit alone: the hybrid has 185 parameters, most of them classical.
  • 16 of the 17 readouts are on data qubits, which start in the input's basis state, so the head sees something close to the pixels.
